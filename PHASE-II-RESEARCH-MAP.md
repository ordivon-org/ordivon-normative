# Ordivon Normative Phase II Research Map

This map names **open frontiers**, not a mandatory backlog. A frontier becomes an active research programme only when its trigger condition is met under `PRESSURE-TO-PROGRAMME-PROTOCOL.md`.

## Frontier A — Rich reason / conflict / defeat semantics

Current baseline: conflict is explicit and non-cancelling; owner-general defeat requires only path-local standing-sufficiency disqualification; richer PHR4 distinctions remain preserved but not promoted.

Potential questions:

- outweighing vs disabling vs undercutting where they produce observably different owner-general consequences;
- reason/support provenance and double-counting;
- support modifiers and dependencies;
- conflict resolution without hidden global priority.

**Trigger:** a concrete Game/Harness/Finance/Human/domain case in which generic path disqualification loses a required semantic distinction, or repeated reason provenance produces a false standing result.

**Do not open for:** philosophical completeness, taxonomy elegance, or desire to import a known argumentation/defeasible-logic framework.

## Frontier B — Temporal branch / retroactivity / reconciliation

Current baseline: cut-indexed history, predecessor/lineage-sensitive ValidChange, evaluation-cut != target-time, retroactivity != history rewrite, interval ontology not required.

Potential questions:

- branch merge/reconciliation;
- competing successor histories;
- annulment vs retroactive ratification;
- branch-local authority migration;
- reopening historical effects after later invalidation.

**Trigger:** a concrete branch/merge case where typed lineage/change claims cannot distinguish two required normative outcomes, or where later retroactive effects create ambiguity not expressible without history rewrite.

**Do not open for:** preference for event sourcing, interval databases, or a total temporal algebra.

## Frontier C — Typed claim grammar

Current baseline: claim vocabulary is open and typed by domain/context; claim terms are not a closed owner ontology.

Potential questions:

- minimal common constructors for Status, Permission, Obligation, Prohibition, Power, ValidChange, Jurisdiction/Recognition;
- claim equivalence vs identity;
- parameter/scope typing;
- explicit opposite/incompatibility relations.

**Trigger:** at least two independent consumer owners require the same claim-level distinction for conformance/interchange and ad hoc opaque terms create an actual ambiguity or false equivalence.

**Do not open for:** serialization convenience or a desire to design a universal Normative AST.

## Frontier D — Context composition / jurisdiction networks

Current baseline: contexts are independent by default; receiving/bridge support is required; no universal merge/precedence/context graph.

Potential questions:

- overlapping jurisdictions;
- competing meta-contexts;
- bridge cycles;
- nested/delegated contexts;
- context birth/death/succession;
- partial comparability.

**Trigger:** a concrete multi-context case where local evaluations plus explicit receiving-context bridge claims cannot preserve governing/translation/currentness distinctions.

**Do not open for:** building a global Ordivon policy superlayer.

## Frontier E — Scope / delegation algebra

Current baseline: bounded delegation is expressible through typed Delta/support qualifications; no universal scope algebra or authority tree is forced.

Potential questions:

- repeated scope narrowing/intersection;
- purpose-limited delegation;
- non-transferability inheritance;
- revocation across independently grounded subdelegation;
- graph provenance under repeated delegation.

**Trigger:** repeated real delegation cases require compositional scope calculations whose correctness cannot be stated or traced with current typed support qualifications.

**Do not open for:** convenience of RBAC/IAM implementation or tree visualization.

## Frontier F — Evidence / support provenance

Current baseline: evidence/provenance != normative standing; Supports != StandingSufficiency; multiple derivations remain first-class.

Potential questions:

- shared upstream evidence reused across many paths;
- provenance-sensitive anti-double-counting;
- evidence currentness vs normative currentness;
- confidence/assurance as non-dispositive support;
- support graph equivalence and explanation completeness.

**Trigger:** a concrete conformance/domain case where multiple apparently independent support paths share a hidden basis and current semantics would incorrectly treat them as independent standing grounds, or where provenance lifecycle changes required standing.

**Do not open for:** building a generic evidence database.

## Frontier G — Representation-plural conformance

Current baseline: generator/history, proposition/state, and transition/judgment-history models all survive; one disposable backend exists.

Potential work:

- a second independent conformance backend;
- model-to-model fixture interchange;
- differential testing across representations.

**Trigger:** evidence that the current witness implementation is biasing fixture design, or a proposed formal-core change whose representation neutrality cannot be assessed from one backend.

This is normally **conformance engineering**, not semantic theory.

## Frontier H — Domain engineering-consumption tests

Possible consumers include Harness, Game, Finance, Human, World, Runtime, Network and future owner-native systems.

Typical questions:

- does a product conflate execution with validity?
- does an `authorized` field collapse Permission/Power/operational admission?
- does currentness derive from latest bytes rather than owner authority?
- does technical identity continuity silently transfer power?
- does one context get merged into another without receiving support?

**Trigger:** actual consumer implementation or design work reaches the relevant boundary.

These are engineering-consumption/dogfood investigations by default. They become Normative theory only if they demonstrate CoreFailure.

## Frontier ordering rule

There is **no canonical A -> B -> C sequence**.

Priority is determined by pressure quality:

1. confirmed CoreFailure pressure;
2. repeated cross-owner BridgePressure;
3. safety/authority-critical ModelPressure;
4. representation/conformance ambiguity;
5. purely conceptual interest.

Only the first four may normally activate Phase II work; purely conceptual interest remains notes unless paired with a falsifiable admission question.
